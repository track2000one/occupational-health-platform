In  an employee occupational health management platform, users should be built on a very important principle in professional systems:
Each user logs in to the platform according to their role only, and can only view or edit the data they are allowed to use.
This is called:
Role-Based Access Control (RBAC
) means a role-based authority system.

1. System Admin / System Administrator
Who is he?
He is the most authoritative user of the platform, and is responsible for the management of the system from a technical and administrative point of view.
His job is in the platform
User management, permissions, general settings, health centers, drop-down menus, activity monitoring, and system protection.
Powers
•	Create user accounts.
•	Modify user data.
•	Deactivate or activate accounts.
•	Assign roles and permissions.
•	Health Center Management.
•	Job Title Management.
•	Manage the types of tests.
•	Manage the types of vaccinations.
•	Manage system settings.
•	View Audit Logs.
•	Follow up on login attempts.
•	Backup management.
•	Export of public reports.
•	Control data access.
Works he does
•	Add a new user such as a doctor or lab administrator.
•	Define the role of the user.
•	Activate or deactivate an employee's account.
•	Review the transaction history to see who edited or deleted data.
•	Adding a new health center.
•	Updating the list of jobs or vaccines.
•	Address minor technical issues within the system.
Important Limitations
Even if the system administrator has broad powers, it is preferable in health systems that he or she can only modify sensitive medical outcomes if he is officially authorized to do so, and any adjustments he makes must be recorded in the operations log.

•	Follow-up of the periodic examination.
•	Follow-up of the results of the tests in general.
•	Follow-up on vaccinations.
•	Follow-up of acupuncture injuries.
•	Following up on the decisions of the Medical Authority.
•	Monitoring the performance of the centers.
•	View reports and indicators.
•	Export management reports.
•	Adopt certain procedures or close cases.
•	Follow-up on delays or shortages.
Works he does
•	Review the coverage of periodic examination in each center.
•	Knowing the positions that are behind in entering the data.
•	Follow-up of employees who have not completed the tests.
•	Follow up on HBsAb Negative or PPD Positive cases as per the work policy.
•	Follow-up of open acupuncture cases.
•	Follow-up of referrals to the medical authority.
•	Issue monthly reports to the Department.
Practical Example
The general supervisor enters the dashboard and sees that a particular center has completed only 45% of the periodic checks, and sends an alert to the center manager or occupational health officer to follow up on the shortage.

Works he does

Instead of just being general, it's best to divide them into levels:
1. Read Validity View
Example:
•	View Employees
•	View Lab Results
•	View Vaccination Records
•	View Reports
2. Validity of the Create plugin 
Example:
•	Create Employee
•	Create Clinic Visit
•	Create Lab Request
•	Create Vaccination Record
3. Update Validity 
Example:
•	Update Employee
•	Update Lab Result
•	Update Referral
•	Update Vaccine Dose

These should be very limited.
It is best in medical systems not to delete permanently, but to use:
Soft Delete
It means the record is not permanently deleted, it just becomes inactive or archived.

Example:
•	Approve Medical Recommendation
•	Approve Committee Decision
•	Approve Imported Excel Data

Important Points to Make the System Suitable for Professional Systems
1. Not giving broad powers to everyone
Each user takes the least validity they just need.
This is called:
Principle of Least Privilege
2. Separation of medical and administrative powers
Not every administrative manager has to see sensitive medical details.
3. Restrict data by center
The director of the center sees only his center, not all the centers.
4. Record Every Process
Every addition, modification, or omission must be recorded in the Audit Logs.
5. No final deletion
It's better to use archiving instead of deletion.
6. Hide sensitive data
Such as:
•	Full ID number.
•	HIV Results .
•	Sensitive diagnostics.
•	Contact numbers.
Except for the authorized user.
7. Validation of important data
Example:
A laboratory result or a medical body decision becomes final only after it has been approved.

Conclusion
Users in the platform should not be one type. It is best to divide them according to the nature of the business:
•	Administrative users.
•	Medical Users.
•	Lab users.
•	Vaccinated users.
•	Medical Practitioner Users.
•	User reports.
•	Regular users.
Each role has its own:
•	Clear function.
•	Specific powers.
•	Specific daily tasks.
•	Limits that do not exceed them.
In this way, the platform is professional, secure, and suitable for a real health system, as it prevents random access to data, maintains employee privacy, and organizes work between different departments.
